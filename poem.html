Always Ask Why

I have always liked to ask "why?"
That question is the only way we can create meaningful change.
Asking the question requires and enables us to sit in discomfort.
Questioning companies, institutions, communities, and beliefs
Even and especially within communities that I identify with.

There is an important distinction between
criticizing people maliciously
versus critically examining
ideas, behaviors, or institutions.
We should be able to oppose prejudice
while still asking difficult questions.

What I Try to Remember

  1. Ask why something works the way it does.
  2. Consider the other perspective before dismissing it.
  3. Criticize ideas not people.
  4. Apply the same standards to groups and institutions I admire.

Questions Worth Asking and Reflecting On

  • Why does disagreement sometimes feel like a personal attack?
  • Why are certain subjects more uncomfortable to criticize than others?
  • Why do dissenting opinions sometimes get shut down?
  • How does avoiding difficult conversations contribute to polarization?
Examine our emotions and reactions
In response to stimuli that elicits discomfort,
I will consider:
Why does this make me uncomfortable?
What can that reaction teach me?

I am not a devil's advocate,
and causing needless turmoil
is just as unhelpful
as blindly accepting the status quo.
It is to remain curious enough to question my own assumptions and intentional
enough to criticize things accurately. I would rather understand why
something happens than accept it because questioning it feels uncomfortable.
That is the mindset I want to carry into the way I learn, work, and contribute
to the world.
